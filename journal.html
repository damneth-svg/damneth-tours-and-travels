First time in Sri Lanka? How to plan a road trip that flows

Sri Lanka looks small on the map, but winding roads mean every drive takes longer than you expect. A little planning makes the whole trip feel easy.

Think in loops, not lines

Most first trips follow a loop: north to the Cultural Triangle, into the hills around Kandy and Ella, down to Yala, then west along the south coast and back to the airport. You never double back, and the beach comes at the end, when you’ll want to slow down.

Two nights beats one

Moving every day is tiring. Two nights in places like Sigiriya, Ella and the coast gives you full days to explore, rather than living out of a suitcase.

Match the coast to the season

The south and west coasts are best from December to April, and the east coast from May to September. We’ll point you to the right side of the island for your dates.

How many days? Seven covers the highlights, ten is a comfortable pace, and fourteen lets you add the east coast or the ancient north. See our sample journeys.

Private driver vs public transport

Sri Lanka’s buses and trains are cheap and part of the adventure. A private driver buys you time, comfort and flexibility. Here’s an honest comparison.

Public transport reaches almost everywhere, but journeys often need a tuk-tuk at each end, and timetables can be unpredictable. With a private driver, the car is waiting when you are ready and stops wherever you like.

Door to doorYes, from the airport to every hotelUsually needs tuk-tuks at each end

If you’re travelling solo on a tight budget with plenty of time, buses and trains are a great way to see local life. Many travellers mix both: a driver for the long road days, and the train to Ella for the views.

What a driver costs with us: multi-day tours start from roughly $78–$120 a day for the vehicle and driver-guide, with no hidden fuel or toll charges.

Best time to visit Sri Lanka

Sri Lanka has two monsoons that reach different coasts at different times of year, so somewhere on the island is almost always in season.

December to April: the west and south coasts

Dry, sunny days on the beaches from Colombo down to Galle and Mirissa, and the clearest views in the hill country. This is also the busiest time of year.

May to September: the east coast

While the south-west monsoon brings rain to the south and west, the east coast around Trincomalee, Pasikudah and Arugam Bay is at its best.

The months in between

October and November, and the weeks around April and May, can bring afternoon thunderstorms across the island. Mornings are often clear, and it’s quieter everywhere.

The Cultural Triangle and the hill country can be visited all year. Tell us your dates and we’ll plan a route that follows the sunshine.

Kandy to Ella train guide

The train ride through tea country is a highlight of any trip to Sri Lanka. A few simple tips make it far more enjoyable.

The full journey from Kandy to Ella takes around six to seven hours. Short on time? Board at Nanu Oya, near Nuwara Eliya, instead. The ride from there to Ella takes about three to four hours and has many of the best views.

Tickets

Reserved seats sell out early in the busy season, so book as soon as your dates are fixed. We can help organise train tickets as part of your trip.

On board

The views are good on both sides. Standing at the open doors makes for great photos, but hold on, and keep your head inside near tunnels and bridges.

Your luggage

With a private driver, your bags travel by road and your driver meets you at the station, so you only need a day bag on the train.

The ride is just as beautiful in the other direction, from Ella back towards Kandy.

Temple etiquette: what to wear and how to visit

Sri Lanka’s temples are living places of worship. A little preparation shows respect and makes every visit easy.

  • Cover shoulders and knees. A light scarf or sarong in your day bag is handy.
  • Take off shoes and hats before entering temple grounds. Socks help on hot stone.
  • Never pose with your back to a Buddha statue for photos, and don’t point your feet towards one.
  • Walk clockwise around dagobas and shrines.
  • Ask first before photographing monks or people at prayer.

Many temples are busiest at puja times, when worshippers bring flowers and offerings. Visiting then is a lovely experience, as long as you keep to the side and stay quiet.

Not sure what’s expected? Ask your driver-guide before you go in.

Kandyan dance and the Esala Perahera

Drums, fire and dazzling costumes. Kandyan dance is one of Sri Lanka’s great art forms, and the Perahera festival is its biggest stage.

Kandyan dance grew from rituals performed in the old hill kingdom. Dancers wear beaded breastplates and silver headdresses, and move to fast, thunderous drumming.

Seeing a show

Cultural shows run most evenings in Kandy and last about an hour. They pair well with the evening puja at the Temple of the Tooth.

The Esala Perahera

Each year, usually in July or August, Kandy holds around ten nights of processions in honour of the Sacred Tooth Relic, with dancers, drummers, fire performers and decorated elephants. Hotels fill up fast, so plan early if your dates line up.

Travelling during the Perahera? Tell us early and we’ll help you find a hotel in or near Kandy.

Four easy days on the south coast

Beaches, a fort town and whales, without any long drives. A gentle way to end a trip.

  1. Galle. Arrive in the afternoon and walk the fort ramparts at sunset.
  2. Weligama. Take a beginner surf lesson, or simply watch the waves.
  3. Mirissa. Whale watching at dawn in season, then sunset at Coconut Tree Hill.
  4. Back to the airport. An easy expressway drive, or stay on longer if you can.

Distances along the south coast are short, so you’ll spend more time on the sand than in the car.

This works best from December to April. Visiting in the summer months? Ask us about the east coast instead.

Two slow days in tea country

The hills reward a slower pace. Here’s how to spend two unhurried days between Nuwara Eliya and Ella.

Day 1: Nuwara Eliya

Wind up through the tea estates, stopping at a waterfall and a working tea factory. Finish with an evening walk by Gregory Lake, and bring a warm layer.

Day 2: The train to Ella

Take the morning train from Nanu Oya and spend the afternoon at the Nine Arches Bridge. Save Little Adam’s Peak for sunrise the next day.

Hill roads are narrow and winding, so we never rush them. Our drivers have a 100% safe driving record.
